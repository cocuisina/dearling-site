1952

Now this is us, nineteen fifty-two, outside the first place we rented. Ines is laughing because I'd said something daft. I couldn't tell you what. We had nothing, mind. A kettle, a bed, a landlady you could hear from the street. But Sundays we took the ferry over to the harbour, threepence each, and felt like millionaires. I can still smell it. Diesel, and wet rope, and chips with far too much vinegar.

1975

Now this one is, what, seventy-six. No. Seventy-five, because Rosa's in that checked frock and she wasn't walking yet. That's Daniel holding her, and Marta, and Marta's mother on the end, who never smiled for a photograph in her life. Look at her now, though. That's Rosa's doing. Daniel carried the basket when he was little, wicker, the handle mended with a bootlace. Oh, I could tell you about that bootlace. No. Now, where was I. Daniel complained the whole way up the hill. Every Sunday.

1994

Marta and her candles. Every birthday, too many on the cake, and Ines would count them out loud. Marta would say, it's a celebration, Ines, not an inspection. Look at Daniel at the back, clapping like he's at the opera. We ate that cake half in the dark, because a bulb had gone and he'd see to it on Saturday. Ninety-four, that was. I believe that bulb was changed in ninety-six.

2008

This is not our harbour. Different shore altogether, but the same grey-blue water. Rosa had brought the children, and a tub of grapes, and far too much watermelon. That's Theo with his face in it, and Mira, nearly three, wearing the rest. I sat there with one on each side of me and I told them about the ferry. And I found I had to stop for a minute. So I ate some watermelon. It's a very good way of not talking.

2025

And this is last year. Or this year, I lose track. Marta and Mira at the dough, Sunday of course. Marta's gone grey and I have not said a word. That rolling pin was Ines's mother's, and it has a crack in it, and nobody will hear of a new one. You'll have noticed Ines isn't in any of the others. She was always holding the camera. That one in fifty-two, I think I tricked her.
